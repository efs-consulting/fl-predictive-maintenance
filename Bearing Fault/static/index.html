BearingFL combines industrial predictive maintenance with privacy-preserving distributed AI — enabling multiple factories and edge devices to collaborate on a shared fault classifier without ever exposing raw sensor data.

The Use Case — Bearing Fault Detection

Rolling element bearings are among the most failure-prone components in rotating machinery — motors, turbines, pumps, and conveyor systems. Bearing failure is responsible for up to 40 % of all rotating machinery breakdowns, often causing costly unplanned downtime, secondary damage, and safety incidents.

Early fault detection through vibration signal analysis allows maintenance engineers to schedule intervention before catastrophic failure. BearingFL uses the Case Western Reserve University (CWRU) benchmark dataset — 1D accelerometer signals sampled at 48 kHz — to classify four bearing conditions:

Normal Healthy baseline — no defect present
Inner Race Defect on the inner raceway surface
Outer Race Defect on the outer raceway surface
Ball Defect on the rolling element itself

Why Federated Learning?

Industrial vibration data is inherently distributed across machines, production lines, and facilities. Centralising this data raises serious barriers: proprietary operational signatures, cross-site confidentiality agreements, high bandwidth costs, and regulations such as GDPR and IEC 62443.

Federated learning eliminates these barriers. Each client trains locally on its own sensor data and sends only model weight updates — never raw signals — to a central aggregation server. The server merges the updates into a global model and distributes it back. Raw data stays on the machine where it was collected.

Key Benefits
Data Privacy
Raw vibration signals never leave the sensor or edge device. Only compressed model gradients traverse the network — satisfying even strict industrial data-sovereignty policies.
Better Generalisation
Training across machines with different load conditions, speeds, and fault severities produces a model that generalises far beyond what any single-site dataset could achieve.
Reduced Bandwidth
Only weight deltas are transmitted — not continuous 48 kHz sensor streams. Communication cost scales with model size, not data volume or sampling rate.
Regulatory Compliance
The federated architecture aligns naturally with GDPR, IEC 62443, and cross-border industrial data-sharing restrictions — no legal grey areas around centralised data lakes.
